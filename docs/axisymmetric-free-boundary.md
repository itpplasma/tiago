# Axisymmetric free-boundary equilibria and magnetic diagnostics

Status: design, 2026-11-19. Nothing below is implemented unless a section
names its module.

Magnetic pickup coils, flux loops and measured coil currents constrain the
plasma current distribution of a tokamak, in particular the edge current
density, which the profile diagnostics of the
[joint fit](recorded-joint-inference.md) barely constrain. This note fixes how
TIAGO predicts these signals from an axisymmetric free-boundary equilibrium,
which existing Grad–Shafranov (GS) codes supply that equilibrium, and how up-down
asymmetric flux labels reach the profile diagnostics. TIAGO does not contain a
GS solver of its own.

## Measurement model

`(R, phi, Z)` are right-handed cylindrical coordinates in m, and currents are
positive along `+phi`. The poloidal flux per radian is `psi = R A_phi`
[Wb/rad], so that

```text
B_R = -(1/R) dpsi/dZ,        B_Z = (1/R) dpsi/dR.                         (1)
```

A toroidal filament of current `I` [A] at `(R', Z')` gives

```text
psi(R, Z) = I G(R, Z; R', Z'),
G = mu0 sqrt(R R') / (2 pi k) [(2 - m) K(m) - 2 E(m)],                   (2)
m = k^2 = 4 R R' / ((R + R')^2 + (Z - Z')^2),
```

with `K(m)`, `E(m)` the complete elliptic integrals of the first and second
kind as functions of the parameter `m` (Jackson, Classical Electrodynamics,
3rd ed., Sec. 5.5). Coil group `g` carries the current `I_g` [A] and consists
of filaments `c` with signed turns `N_c`. A coil of finite cross-section is a
set of Gauss–Legendre filaments whose `N_c` are its turns times the quadrature
weights. With the toroidal plasma current density `j_phi` [A/m^2],

```text
psi = sum_g I_g sum_{c in g} N_c G(.; R_c, Z_c) + int_P G(.; R', Z') j_phi dR' dZ',  (3)
j_phi = R p'(psi) + F F'(psi) / (mu0 R),                                  (4)
```

where `P` is the plasma region bounded by the last closed flux surface, `p` the
pressure [Pa] and `F = R B_phi` [T m]. The sensors are:

```text
full flux loop at (R, Z):     s = 2 pi psi(R, Z)                                [Wb]
pickup coil:                  s = N A (1/L) int_{-L/2}^{L/2} B(x + l t) . n dl  [Wb]
Rogowski coil, contour C:     s = I_P + sum_{c inside C} N_c I_g(c)             [A]
coil current measurement:     s = I_g                                           [A]
```

The pickup coil has its centre at `x`, its axis along the unit vector `n` in
the poloidal plane, its length `L` along the unit vector `t` in the same plane,
and the effective area `N A` [m^2] from its calibration. `l` is the arc length
along `t` and `I_P` the plasma current. The pickup voltage and digitiser
follow from `s` through the existing [pickup](magnetic-pickup.md) and
[digitiser](digitizer.md) stages. Given `j_phi`, every signal is linear in the
`I_g`, so the coil part is a response matrix, as for the existing
[stellarator sensors](magnetics.md#magnetic-probes-and-response-matrices).

Equation (3) needs no field grid and holds at any sensor position, inside or
outside the computational box of the GS code. Two independent checks fix its
accuracy:

1. Filaments against TIAGO's straight-segment Biot–Savart kernel on a polygon
   of the same circle.
2. For a Solov'ev equilibrium (Solov'ev, Sov. Phys. JETP 26, 400 (1968)) with
   `psi = 0` on its boundary, the plasma term of (3) outside `P` equals the
   flux of the toroidal sheet current `K_phi = (n x B)_phi / mu0` on the
   boundary, with `n` its outward unit normal (the axisymmetric virtual
   casing). The total current equals `(1/mu0)` times the circulation of the
   poloidal field around the boundary.

`tiago_axisym_magnetics` (`src/solver/axisym_magnetics.f90`) implements (2),
the coil part of (3) and the flux-loop, pickup and Rogowski signals for any
filament set. `axisym_rectangle` turns a rectangular coil into filaments and
`response` gives the signals per unit group current. The kernel of (1) and (2)
is generated by `gen/gen_axisym_green.f90`, with `dK/dm` and `dE/dm` written in
`K` and `E`. The test `tiago_axisym_magnetics` runs checks 1 and 2:

- `psi`, `B_R`, `B_Z` agree with polygons of 8192 and 16384 sides,
  extrapolated in the side count, to `1e-10` relative, at points down to
  5 cm from the filament.
- On the Solov'ev region `c1 (R^2 - 1)^2 / 8 + 0.4 Z^2 < 0.0595` with
  `c1 = 1`, the volume integral (40 x 256 points) and the boundary sheet
  (256 points) give the same signals to `1e-11` relative, and the total
  current matches its closed form to `1e-11`.

## Equilibrium providers

| code | boundary | output read by TIAGO | role |
|---|---|---|---|
| [FreeGS](https://github.com/freegs-plasma/freegs) (or its successor FreeGSNKE) | free, filament and polygonal coils | G-EQDSK | first provider and published test machine |
| MILCA `nested_equilibrium`, our nested-map MHD equilibrium code, axisymmetric case | free, in the field of an mgrid | `psi(R, Z)` or boundary and profiles, export still to be added in MILCA | second provider, with a Newton tangent in the coil currents |
| [CHEASE](https://gitlab.epfl.ch/spc/chease) | fixed | EQDSK | interior on the boundary of another provider |
| VMEC++ (present in TIAGO) | free, mgrid | VMEC `wout` file | 3D equilibria with coils on (ripple, perturbation coils), not the axisymmetric fit |

G-EQDSK is the EFIT equilibrium file: `psi` on an `(R, Z)` grid, `p`, `F`,
`p'`, `F F'` and `q` on `psi_N`, the last closed surface and the limiter. An
mgrid is the MAKEGRID file of the field of each coil group on a cylindrical
grid, read by VMEC. FreeGS is Python and stays an external process. No
provider becomes a library dependency of TIAGO.

### Provider contract

The provider seam of [DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md#forward-provider-seam) is
realised as a process with a file contract:

- Request: coil-group currents `I_g` [A], plasma current `I_P` [A], the shape
  parameters of `p'` and `F F'` as piecewise cubic Hermite knots in `psi_N`,
  a boundary guess and the solver tolerances. `I_P` fixes the common scale of
  `p'` and `F F'`. Each backend converts the knots into its own profile
  representation.
- Response: G-EQDSK with its COCOS index, the tokamak coordinate convention
  of Sauter and Medvedev (Comput. Phys. Commun. 184, 293 (2013)), the
  convergence status, the solver residual and the provider name, version and
  command line.

TIAGO reads the G-EQDSK with libneo's reader and evaluates (3) and (4)
itself. It takes the file's current as flowing along `+phi` and converts its
flux as `psi = sigma psi_file / (2 pi)^e`, with `e = 0` or `1` chosen so that
the integral of `j_phi` matches that current and `sigma` from their signs.
This covers every COCOS of a file whose current sign follows `+phi`. The
integral in (3) runs over the grid nodes inside the last closed surface with
weight `dR dZ`, the quadrature FreeGS uses. A provider may add its own
`j_phi` on the grid as a sidecar file, which then replaces (4) and the
boundary test. Planned: the provider's own `psi` is compared with (3) at the
grid points outside `P` and the difference is recorded with each solve.
Different coils, a COCOS or `2 pi` mismatch and provider resolution each
produce such a difference.

`tiago_geqdsk_plasma` (`src/solver/geqdsk_plasma.f90`) implements the reading,
the conversion, `j_phi` and the labels (5) and (6). `tiago_axisym_machine`
reads a machine file of coils, flux loops, pickups and Rogowski coils. The
test `tiago_geqdsk_plasma` compares them with FreeGS on its
`TestTokamakSensor` machine (`tests/freegs/generate_test_tokamak.py`, 65 x 65
grid, up-down asymmetric diverted plasma of 200 kA). The references take the
plasma field from FreeGS's Green's functions of its own current:

| quantity | largest difference | scale |
|---|---|---|
| plasma current | 0.37 A | 200 kA |
| flux loops | 1.1e-6 Wb | 0.29 Wb |
| pickup field | 1.5e-6 T | 0.16 T |
| `psi_N` at 24 points | 2.9e-7 | 1 |
| `rho_tor` at 5 labels | 7.7e-5 | 1 |

FreeGS writes its G-EQDSK profiles on `psi_N = i / n` instead of
`i / (n - 1)` ([freegs#163](https://github.com/freegs-plasma/freegs/issues/163)),
so the generator rewrites them on the standard grid. FreeGS's grid flux of the
plasma differs from the Green's-function sum of its own current by 1e-4 to
3e-4 Wb/rad, depending on the point and not decreasing from 65 to 129 grid
points. This changes its flux-loop values by up to about 1 %.

### Cell quadrature for continuous currents

The integral in (3) over grid nodes is exact for a provider whose current lives
on the nodes, as in FreeGS and FreeGSNKE. For a continuous current (an analytic
equilibrium, CHEASE) its error is of the order of the cells the boundary cuts:
on the generic tokamak of the `tests/analytic` fixtures the plasma part of flux
loops and pickups is off by `1e-3` at 65 x 65 to 129 x 129 points. With
`cell_quadrature = .true.` TIAGO instead integrates `j_phi` with 2 x 2 Gauss
points in each cell inside the plasma, and in each cell the boundary cuts with
`n_sub x n_sub` sub-cells (default 16), each weighted with its area fraction
below `psi_N = 1` for a linear `psi_N`. Near the boundary `psi_N` decides
whether a point is plasma, and the last closed surface polygon only excludes
the private flux region: a point within one polygon segment of it counts as
plasma when its straight path to the magnetic axis stays below `psi_N = 1`.

The tests `tiago_geqdsk_cells_limited` and `tiago_geqdsk_cells_xpoint` compare
the plasma current and eight sensors with Gauss quadrature of the exact current
density of Cerfon–Freidberg equilibria (Phys. Plasmas 17, 032502 (2010);
`tests/analytic/generate.py`): a limited case at 65 x 65 to `1e-5` and a single
null at 129 x 129 to `5e-5` of the largest signal. The error falls to `1e-7`
at 257 x 257 points in both topologies.

### FreeGSNKE provider

FreeGS's Picard iteration does not hold an elongated plasma at fixed coil
currents. Started from the `TestTokamakSensor` solution with its own coil
currents, it finds no O-point after the current of coil `P2U` changes by
+1 %, and after -2 % its axis moves from `Z = 0.04 m` to `Z = 0.88 m`. The
forward provider therefore uses the Newton–Krylov static solver of
[FreeGSNKE](https://github.com/FusionComputingLab/freegsnke) 3.1.1 at fixed coil
currents. `tools/freegsnke_provider.py` reads a request file of `key = value`
lines (machine file, grid, tolerance, `plasma_current`, `pressure_axis`,
`fvac`, `alpha_m`, `alpha_n`, `current(g)`), builds the coils from the same
machine file (a rectangle as 4 x 4 midpoint filaments, where TIAGO uses 4 x 4
Gauss–Legendre filaments), solves from FreeGSNKE's default initial flux and
writes the G-EQDSK, its `j_phi` sidecar and a status file. The profiles are
FreeGSNKE's `ConstrainPaxisIp` family. Hermite knots of `p'` and `F F'`
follow with layer 6.

`tiago_axisym_free_boundary` (`src/inference/axisym_free_boundary.f90`)
implements the posterior contract of the inference engines with parameters
`CURRENT(g)`, `PLASMA_CURRENT` and `PRESSURE_AXIS`, Gaussian priors from
measured coil currents and re-solved central differences. A failed solve is
repeated once, and a derivative falls back to one side where the other does
not converge.

The test `tiago_axisym_free_boundary` (label `slow`, enabled with
`TIAGO_FREEGSNKE_PYTHON` set to a Python that imports `freegsnke`) plants an
equilibrium on `TestTokamakSensor` (33 x 33 grid), predicts its six flux
loops, six pickups and the Rogowski coil without noise
(`sigma` = 1e-4 Wb, 1e-4 T, 50 A) and measures the coil currents to 1 %.
From a start 0.3 % off in the coil currents, 2 % in `I_P` and 20 % in the
axis pressure, Levenberg–Marquardt converges in 4 steps and 90 provider solves
(4 min on one core):

| parameter | planted | fitted | local standard deviation |
|---|---|---|---|
| `P1L` current | 153020 A | 153020 A | 59 A |
| `P1U` current | 62716 A | 62716 A | 54 A |
| `P2L` current | -98061 A | -98061 A | 26 A |
| `P2U` current | -57558 A | -57558 A | 30 A |
| `I_P` | 200000 A | 200000 A | 49 A |
| axis pressure | 1000.0 Pa | 999.99 Pa | 23 Pa |

Central differences with relative steps `1e-3` (the default) and `3e-4`
differ by `2.5e-3` in the `P2U` column and by `1e-4` to `6e-4` in the others.
The test requires `5e-3`.

The test requires 5e-4 in the current, 1e-3 m at the axis, 2e-3 in `psi_N`,

### Profile knots and the command-line fit

With `profile = 'knots'` the provider takes `p'` and `F F'` as natural cubic
splines through values at knots in `psi_N` (FreeGSNKE's
`GeneralPprimeFFprime`) and scales both together to `I_P`. One knot value
stays fixed to remove that common scale. The parameters `PPRIME(k)` and
`FFPRIME(k)` are the knot values. Two kinds of data rows join the sensors:
the pressure `p(psi_N)` [Pa] from the G-EQDSK, and `j_phi` [A/m^2] at the
outboard point of `psi_N` on the horizontal line through the axis. The second
serves as prior or pseudo-measurement of the edge current, for example a
bootstrap estimate. MSE pi-polarization angles ([MSE](mse.md)) follow as
further rows, with the field of the G-EQDSK

```text
B_R = -(1/R) dpsi/dZ,   B_Z = (1/R) dpsi/dR,   B_phi = h |F(psi_N)| / R,   h = sign(F I_P),   (4a)
```

with `F` of the boundary outside the plasma. The helicity `h` is read from the
file, where `F` and `I_P` share the toroidal direction in every COCOS.

FreeGSNKE's Newton-Krylov solve does not converge from its default initial
flux for every request. A solve therefore starts from a fixed plasma flux
(`initial`, the `.npy` the provider writes next to each G-EQDSK), by default
the one of the first converged solve of the fit. A solve that fails twice
from it is tried once more from FreeGSNKE's default initial flux. A plasma
without X-point, which FreeGSNKE's G-EQDSK writer cannot write, counts as a
failed solve. A derivative whose solve fails shrinks its step twice by 4, then
falls back to one side. The
Newton-Krylov residual stalls at a floor that depends on the request: in the
knot fit of `tests/axisym_fit.py` one derivative solve of `PPRIME(3)` ends at
a relative residual of `2.2e-6` after 400 iterations, and continuing from that
state does not lower it. The test therefore accepts `3e-6`.

FreeGSNKE carries the current on grid nodes inside the boundary. Where
`j_phi` at `psi_N = 1` is not zero, nodes entering and leaving the plasma make
the sensor signals jump with the parameters. In the knot fit of
`tests/axisym_fit.py` with `p' = 500 Pa/(Wb/rad)` and `F F' = 0.002 T^2 m^2/(Wb/rad)`
at the boundary, a scan of `I_P` in steps of `5e-5` gives second differences of
the flux-loop signals of up to `8e-4 Wb`, against `2e-5 Wb` with zero
boundary values. Such jumps stall the fit, so knot values at `psi_N = 1`
stay at zero.

`tiago_axisym_fit input.nml` runs the whole fit from a namelist: machine file,
provider command, measurements by sensor label plus `pressure@x` and `jphi@x`
rows, MSE channels, coil currents, knots, parameters with scales and Gaussian priors. It
writes the MAP with local standard deviations, measured and predicted
signals, the MAP G-EQDSK, G-EQDSKs of Laplace draws re-solved by the provider
and the outboard `j_phi` at chosen `psi_N` for the MAP and every draw. With
`predict_geqdsk` it only predicts the sensors of a given G-EQDSK.

### Derivatives

The coil part is exact and linear. Derivatives with respect to the
equilibrium parameters (`I_g`, `I_P`, profile knots) are re-solved central
differences, as in the VMEC++ free-boundary path
([reconstruction](reconstruction.md#free-boundary-provider)), two solves per
parameter. Every solve starts from the same plasma flux, so the forward map
does not depend on the order of the solves. A provider tangent, such as MILCA's derivative of the free-boundary
state with respect to the plasma current, replaces these differences parameter
by parameter after a check against them.

The edge current enters through the knot values of `p'` and `F F'` near
`psi_N = 1`. The pickup coils near the boundary constrain them, and a
bootstrap-current prior (Sauter, Angioni and Lin-Liu, Phys. Plasmas 6, 2834
(1999)) supplies their prior.

## Up-down asymmetric flux labels

The G-EQDSK flux `psi(R, Z)` carries no up-down symmetry. The profile labels
are

```text
psi_N(R, Z) = (psi(R, Z) - psi_axis) / (psi_b - psi_axis),               (5)
Phi(psi_N) = 2 pi |psi_b - psi_axis| int_0^psi_N |q| dpsi_N',   rho_tor = sqrt(Phi(psi_N) / Phi(1)),  (6)
```

with bicubic interpolation of `psi` and `psi_b` the flux of the last closed
surface. `Phi` [Wb] is the toroidal flux, integrated from the tabulated `q`.
At the separatrix of a diverted plasma `q` diverges logarithmically, so
`Phi(1)` depends on how the provider tabulates its last point. Profile diagnostics take `psi_N` or `rho_tor` at
their sample points instead of the VMEC label `s`. The derivative of a label at
a fixed point with respect to a parameter includes the shifts of `psi_axis`
and `psi_b`.

`psi_b` is the flux of the X-point for a diverted plasma and of the limiter
contact point for a limited one, both reported by the provider. Points outside
`P` carry only `psi_N`: those with `psi_N > 1` lie in the scrape-off layer,
and those with `psi_N < 1` below the X-point in the private flux region. VMEC
labels cannot describe either region. Up-down asymmetric VMEC++ states for the
3D path (`lasym = true` in TIAGO's VMEC++ state and the joint model) are a
separate item.

## Profile diagnostics on G-EQDSK labels

`tiago_geqdsk_state` fills the provider rows of the profile-diagnostic contract
([inference](inference.md#profile-diagnostic-interface)) from a G-EQDSK: the
normalized toroidal flux `s = Phi(psi_N) / Phi(1)` of (6), with `Phi` between
the table points a cubic Hermite interpolant whose slopes are
`2 pi |psi_b - psi_axis| |q|`, so that `s` and `ds/dpsi_N` are consistent,
continued linearly beyond `psi_N = 1`; and `|B| = sqrt(|grad psi|^2 + F^2) / R`,
with the `F` of the boundary outside the plasma. Every existing profile
diagnostic (point probes, line integrals, Thomson, ...) then works on an
up-down asymmetric equilibrium without a VMEC state.

`tiago_profile_posterior` is the posterior of the kinetic profiles in such a
fixed equilibrium, with an alignment and a log-scale calibration per
diagnostic and the exact Jacobian through the diagnostic VJPs. The test
`tiago_profile_posterior` checks that Jacobian against central differences and
recovers planted profile, alignment and calibration latents from Thomson-like
and charge-exchange-like points with 3 % noise on the analytic limited
equilibrium of `tests/analytic`.

On the analytic equilibria, `s` agrees with the contour-integral reference to
`2e-8` and its gradient to `2e-8` at 129 x 129 points for a limited plasma, and
`|B|` to `2e-10`. In a diverted plasma `s` is off by `5e-5` to `1e-4` because
`q` diverges at the separatrix and the table holds a finite last value.

## Files shared with MILCA

TIAGO and MILCA read one machine description and never copy it:

- Coil filaments: one coil file, ordered by group, with signed turns per
  filament and the current per unit `EXTCUR`, VMEC's coil-group current.
- Field basis: the mgrid with `mgrid_mode = 'S'` (field of group `k` per unit
  `EXTCUR_k`), produced once from that coil file by Biot–Savart. MILCA and
  VMEC++ read this file. Coil file and mgrid agree in group order, sign and
  current unit.
- Equilibria: G-EQDSK, MILCA's NetCDF output and VMEC `wout` files stay in the
  study directory of the private device repository that owns the discharge.
  TIAGO records path, SHA-256 and producer in its provenance.

MILCA requires a positive toroidal flux. For a machine with negative toroidal
field the adapter passes all coil-group currents and the plasma current
negated and negates the returned `psi`, so that TIAGO's signals keep their
physical signs.

## Test cases

Tests use synthetic or published machines only.

- FreeGS `TestTokamakSensor` at a pinned FreeGS revision: four coils, a wall,
  a Rogowski coil, pickup coils and flux loops. A G-EQDSK and FreeGS's own
  sensor values, generated once and checked in with their generator, test
  (3) and (4) against an independent code.
- MILCA's synthetic free-boundary tokamak
  (`examples/nested-equilibrium/free-boundary-tokamak.nml` with its analytic
  mgrid) and its converged VMEC++ reference.
- A machine of circular filaments only, solved by FreeGS and by MILCA from the
  same coil file and mgrid, with the FreeGS boundary re-solved by CHEASE,
  compares axis, volume, internal inductance and the sensor signals across
  codes.

Device coils, probes and discharges stay in the private device repositories.
