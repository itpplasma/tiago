# Doppler reflectometry

A Doppler reflectometer measures the angular frequency shift of scattered
microwaves. The local operator takes the signed Bragg wavenumber \(k\) in
\(\mathrm{m^{-1}}\), its unit direction \(\hat{\boldsymbol k}\), a radial electric
field \(E_r\) in \(\mathrm{V\,m^{-1}}\), an outward unit normal \(\hat{\boldsymbol n}\)
and the magnetic field \(\boldsymbol B\) in tesla. The scattering layer and
wavenumber come from ray tracing. A nuisance velocity \(v_\mathrm{ph}\) describes
the propagation of the scattering fluctuations along \(\hat{\boldsymbol k}\).

\[
 B_\mathrm{proj} = \hat{\boldsymbol k}\cdot(\hat{\boldsymbol n}\times\boldsymbol B),
 \qquad
 \omega_D = k \left(E_r\,\frac{B_\mathrm{proj}}{|\boldsymbol B|^2}
                      + v_\mathrm{ph}\right).
\]

`tiago_doppler_reflectometry::doppler_reflectometry` returns \(\omega_D\) in rad/s
and its gradient in \(E_r, v_\mathrm{ph}, k, |\boldsymbol B|^2, B_\mathrm{proj}\).
Pass \(2\pi f_D\) for a measured shift \(f_D\) in Hz. The field squared must be
positive. The predicted shift has zero electric-field sensitivity when either
\(k\) or \(B_\mathrm{proj}\) is zero.

An electric-field fit needs a constraint on the phase velocity and the
scattering-layer position. To compare a predicted shift with an observation,
propagate the ray and magnetic covariance through the returned gradient,
including covariance between \(k\), \(B_\mathrm{proj}\) and \(|\boldsymbol B|^2\).
The caller supplies those quantities and the receiver frequency convention.
The local scattering approximation follows [Conway et al. (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1088/0029-5515/46/9/S15).

## Ray to the turning point

`tiago_doppler_ray` supplies the scattering point and wavenumber from a cold
ray of the [ECE ray primitive](ece-rays.md) in O or X mode. With the ray state
\((\boldsymbol x,\boldsymbol N)\) and Hamiltonian \(H\), the turning point is
the time \(t_*\) at which \(|\boldsymbol N|\) is minimal,

\[
 g(t_*) = \boldsymbol N\cdot\dot{\boldsymbol N} = -\boldsymbol N\cdot H_{\boldsymbol x} = 0 .
\]

Backscattering there selects the fluctuation wavevector
\(\boldsymbol q = -2k_0\boldsymbol N_\perp\), \(k_0 = 2\pi f/c\), with
\(\boldsymbol N_\perp\) the part of \(\boldsymbol N\) perpendicular to
\(\boldsymbol B\). The outward normal is \(\hat{\boldsymbol n} = -\nabla n_e/|\nabla n_e|\).
The shift of the operator above becomes

\[
 \omega_D = -2k_0\left(E_r\,\frac{\boldsymbol N\cdot(\hat{\boldsymbol n}\times\boldsymbol B)}{B^2}
            + v_\mathrm{ph}\,|\boldsymbol N_\perp|\right).
\]

`find_doppler_turning` traces the ray over a caller duration that must pass
the turning point, brackets the sign change of \(g\) on the accepted steps and
retraces to \(t_*\) by safeguarded Newton steps. `doppler_ray_shift` returns
\(\omega_D\) and the signed wavenumber \(-2k_0|\boldsymbol N_\perp|\).
`doppler_ray_shift_jvp` propagates tangents of the launch state, medium
parameters, frequency, \(E_r\) and \(v_\mathrm{ph}\). It adds the moving
turning point to the fixed-time ray tangent,

\[
 \delta t_* = -\frac{\partial_{\boldsymbol y} g\cdot\delta\boldsymbol y + \partial_p g\,\delta p}
                    {\partial_{\boldsymbol y} g\cdot\dot{\boldsymbol y}},
 \qquad
 \delta\boldsymbol y_* = \delta\boldsymbol y + \dot{\boldsymbol y}\,\delta t_*,
\]

and the field and density-gradient changes at the moved point. The kernel is
generated by `gen/gen_reflectometry.f90`.

Benchmark: in a slab with \(X = gx\), \(\boldsymbol B\) along \(z\) and a
launch at tilt \(\theta\) from the gradient, the poloidal index
\(K/k_0 = \sin\theta\) is conserved, the ray turns at \(X = \cos^2\theta\) and
backscattering selects \(q = -2K\), so the shift is \(qv\) with
\(v = E_r/B + v_\mathrm{ph}\)
([Gusakov and Surkov 2004](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0410106), Eqs. (1)
and (5)). `tiago_doppler_ray` reproduces the turning point to \(9\times10^{-16}\) m
and the wavenumber and shift to \(10^{-8}\) relative for
\(\sin\theta = 0.1\ldots0.4\). Tangents of an oblique O- and X-mode ray in a
sheared field and curved density agree with central differences to
\(4\times10^{-6}\) relative. Normal incidence reaches \(\boldsymbol N = 0\),
where the magnetized ray is singular, so a zero tilt is outside the domain.

The geometric-optics turning point is not the full-wave scattering
location, and the spectral width from the finite beam (Gusakov and Surkov,
Eq. (5)) is not modelled.
